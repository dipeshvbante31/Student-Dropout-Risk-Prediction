About this project

Purpose

A decision-support application that estimates the risk of student dropout so staff can offer help earlier. It is a machine-learning project first: the web interface delivers a trained, evaluated model.

Machine-learning approach

  • Dataset: "Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success" (UCI Machine Learning Repository, id 697; Realinho et al., 2022; CC BY 4.0): 4,424 students of a Portuguese higher-education institution, 36 features, outcome Dropout, Enrolled or Graduate.
  • Prediction problem: binary. Dropout = 1; Graduate and Enrolled = 0. "Enrolled" students have not yet finished, so they are treated as not (yet) dropped out.
  • Pipeline: feature engineering (approval rates, grade and approval change between semesters), median/most-frequent imputation, scaling, one-hot encoding, then the classifier, all serialized as one scikit-learn pipeline reused for form and CSV predictions.
  • Models compared: logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting, by 5-fold cross-validation on the training split; selection by F1 for the dropout class (ROC-AUC as tie-break). Metrics are reported once on a 20% held-out test set.
  • Explanations: global permutation importance and a per-student occlusion analysis. They describe the model, not causes.

Limitations and ethics

  • The data come from one institution and period; performance on other populations is unknown and may be lower.
  • Predictions use first- and second-semester results, so they are most useful after the first year begins.
  • The dataset includes demographic attributes. The model page shows a group check, but that does not make the model free of bias.
  • Risk levels (low below {{ low_pct }}%, medium below {{ med_pct }}%) are communication thresholds, not universal standards.
  • Never use a prediction to expel, suspend, penalise, deny admission or make disciplinary decisions. Use it to start a supportive conversation.
